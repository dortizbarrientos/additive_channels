How the figure is calculated

The coordinates have been turned so the horizontal direction u points from the population mean towards the fitness peak and v runs across the slope. They are measured in the same units as the original breeding values. The fixed surface is ℓ = −0.4[(u − )² + v²] (peak distance rounded here). The population mean is (0,0); the variances along the long and short axes are 0.20 and 0.005. Both stay fixed.

For angle φ, the variance along the uphill direction is 0.20 cos²φ + 0.005 sin²φ. Multiply by the squared uphill slope, 0.6525, to obtain the linear variance. The curvature variance is 0.32(0.20² + 0.005²) = 0.012808 at every angle. Divide linear variance by their sum to obtain the index. These values use the full Gaussian distribution. Turning the same 225 sampled points illustrates how its orientation changes.

The fitted plane has the slope of the landscape at the mean. Its intercept is lower than the tangent plane's by 0.082 log units, because the best fit averages curvature across parents. The outline contains 90% of the Gaussian population. With expected-offspring weighting selected, dot area is proportional to exp(ℓ); the dots remain at the same positions. These are expected reproductive weights. No offspring counts or inheritance process are simulated.
